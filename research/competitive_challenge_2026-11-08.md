## Critical challenge
The proposed $149 audit overlaps a competitor's free audit. Selling the same output at $149 is weak positioning. The only plausible distinction is hands-on **backlog cleanup**: owner-approved triage of ten neglected estimates, reconciliation of unclear statuses, prioritized action list, tailored drafts and handoff. This distinction remains hypothetical and needs a buyer test. Avoid promising recovered revenue.

## Owner-of-capital decision
Spend $0 of the hypothetical $500 on software, ads, domains or subscriptions until buyer evidence. Set a maximum of one focused validation cycle: 5 qualified, consenting buyer conversations; >=3 describe costly backlog pain; >=2 express concrete willingness to pay $149 for backlog cleanup rather than a free audit. If this fails, stop/pivot rather than invest in the app.

## Execution sequence
1. Build a polished synthetic backlog-cleanup example with specific deliverables and clear suppression rules.
2. Time the work; target <=90 minutes of total delivery effort.
3. Find permission-based routes to buyers and request approval before outreach, advertising, sign-ups or collecting data.
4. Record anonymous evidence; make go/no-go decision.
