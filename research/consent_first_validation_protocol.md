# Consent-first validation protocol — 10-Quote Recovery Review

**Experiment status (2026-11-08):** Hypothesis only. $0 spent, $0 earned, no interviews or customer commitments verified. No outreach or public advertising is authorized.

## Buyer and purchase trigger

Owner-operator of a 1–5-person residential remodeling business who has at least 10 outstanding quotes, currently tracks them in a spreadsheet/inbox, and personally decides whether to follow up. Exclude shops with a mature CRM follow-up workflow, commercial bid teams, and any buyer who expects us to contact their customers.

**Test offer:** A one-time **$149** review of up to 10 sanitized open estimates: priority triage, owner-approval follow-up drafts, and a 15-minute handoff. No subscriptions, conversion guarantees, automated sending, or CRM migration.

## Permission gate (before any outreach)

The operator must explicitly approve the specific channel, target group, and exact message before anyone posts, sends DMs, emails, calls, or recruits interviewees. No scraped contacts, purchased lists, mass unsolicited messages, or pretending to be a customer. A consenting referral/introduction or an approved community post is preferred. Do not take payment or accept identifiable client data until a secure workflow and delivery terms are approved.

## Five-question discovery conversation (only with consenting owners)

1. Walk me through what happens after you send an estimate. Where do you track it?
2. How many sent estimates have not received a final yes/no? How long have they been sitting?
3. What have you tried to follow up, and what makes it hard?
4. What would you expect to receive from someone who reviews 10 of those quotes for you, without contacting your clients?
5. If that deliverable were ready this week, would you pay $149 once for it? What would stop you? (Do not treat polite praise as intent to buy.)

Do not pitch the price until after questions 1–4. Ask for permission to record anonymized notes, not audio by default.

## Demonstration script

Show the synthetic CSV and generated audit. Explain that every quoted dollar is **potential pipeline**, not recovered revenue. Walk through a due quote, a replied quote, a completed quote, and a do-not-contact quote. Show an owner-editable draft for one eligible quote, then explain the owner—not the service—decides whether and how to send it.

## Evidence ledger (no personal information)

For each consenting conversation record: anonymous interview ID, qualification yes/no, number of outstanding quotes (band), current tracking method, follow-up pain in their own words, current workaround, reaction to $149, explicit willingness to buy yes/no/unclear, and disqualifying objections. Do not store names, phone numbers, addresses, customer records, or unredacted transcripts in GitHub.

**Threshold to proceed:** 5 qualified conversations; >=3 with meaningful, specific pain; >=2 explicit $149 purchase commitments. If unmet, revise the segment/offer once and retest; do not invest in a full SaaS. No actual demand has yet been demonstrated.

## Delivery and safety requirements before first paid pilot

- Written scope, refund/turnaround policy, owner sign-off, and explicit exclusion of legal/contract advice.
- Approved secure intake and deletion policy for any client information; prefer IDs, job category, quote amount, dates, and status only.
- Every draft must be reviewed by the owner for factual accuracy, opt-out, prior responses, and communication consent.
- No outbound communication to homeowner leads by the service.
- Separate business transaction/payment mechanism only after explicit authorization.
